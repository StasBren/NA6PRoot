What this page computes

An avalanche leaves a cloud of positive ions a few micrometres from the anode wire. Each ion drifts to a cathode and, while it moves, induces charge on the cathode strips (Shockley–Ramo: induced charge = ion charge × change of the strip's weighting potential along the path). The strip signal of a cloud is the sum over its ions.

The page contains a precomputed single-ion response table: the induced charge on a strip, versus time, for one ion born at radius r and angle θ around the central anode and at strip offset D. It was computed for this page with an independent re-implementation of the electric field and the ion drift (same 9-wire 2100 V cell, same provisional Ar⁺ mobility table) and the frozen weighting-potential table of the full calculation; this re-implementation reproduces the Garfield++-based response to 0.3 %. Grid: 10 radii 15.2–70 µm, 72 angles (5°), D in 0.17 mm steps, 48 times 0–7 µs.

Three (or four) signals are built from the same ions:

  • Ion by ion (black): every ion looked up in the table at its own r and θ and summed. This plays the role of the full calculation.
  • One radius only (grey dotted): every ion moved to the cloud's r_eff, keeping its own angle. Its difference to ion by ion is the cost of describing the radii by one number.
  • Ten numbers, K2A (red dashed): only the cloud's ten numbers are used, exactly as in the K2A formula
      Q(τ) = N [K₀ + C₁Kc₁ + S₁Ks₁ + C₂Kc₂ + S₂Ks₂ + β K_A] at r_eff and D,
    i.e. 72 angle nodes at r_eff weighted with am = (1/72)[1 + 2Σn≤2(Cn cos nθm + Sn sin nθm)] + β s⊥m, where s⊥ is the part of sign(sin θ) orthogonal to the kept harmonics and β = (A − Σa⁰m sign sin θm)/(s⊥·sign sin θ) restores the exact up/down split.
  • K2 (orange, optional): the same without the A term (β = 0), to show what the hemisphere term does.

The difference between "one radius only" and "ten numbers" is the angular compression (harmonics up to n = 2 plus the hemisphere term).

How to use it

  1. Start with the preset Typical avalanche: a compact cloud on the upper side, 2–3 µm above the wire. On the charge plot all curves lie on top of each other and the shaped differences are about 0.2 %. The plane charges at 7 µs, however, differ by 0.6–0.9 % in opposite directions on U and V: a single compact cloud is the hardest case for the angular truncation (box 2 below).
  2. Pick a strip with the buttons above the plots: U0 is the strip directly above the cloud, U±1/U±2 its neighbours, V0… the strips on the opposite cathode, "U plane"/"V plane" the sums.
  3. Change one slider at a time and watch the difference plot. New random sample draws a new set of ions with the same settings, which shows how much is just sampling noise.
  4. Compare the log-time charge plot (early times magnified) with the shaped plot (what an amplifier with peaking time Tp would see).

What is lost, and how to see it

1. The spread of radii inside a cloud. K2A keeps only r_eff = exp⟨ln r⟩. Preset Large radial spread: the grey dotted and red dashed curves leave the black one in the first 10–100 ns, by several percent on the log-time plot. Reason: early on, an ion's signal depends on its own birth radius in a non-linear way; only once it has moved far from its birth point does the signal depend on ln r linearly, and then the log average is exact. After shaping, the difference shrinks, because the shaper averages over ~Tp.
2. Angular detail beyond the second harmonic. K2A keeps C₁, S₁ (direction) and C₂, S₂ (elongation), plus the exact up/down split A. A compact cloud has large higher harmonics: a 15°-wide lobe has |C₃, S₃| ≈ 0.75, listed under "not contained". K2A cannot place such a cloud exactly in angle, and at 7 µs this moves charge between the U and V planes by up to about 1 % for a single compact cloud (compare "one radius only" with "ten numbers" in the summary). Preset Three lobes shows the same effect for a strongly structured cloud.

Open question: is this the origin of the near-limit window-charge and U/V-ratio differences seen in real muons? In five reference muons the angular truncation changed the overall signal scale by only ±0.03 %, while the one-radius approximation changed it by −0.1 to −0.2 %; but that scale is dominated by the early current, not by the window charge. A window-charge breakdown of muon 0740 (approximation by approximation) is the test.
3. The up/down split without A. Preset At the side (cloud near θ = 0°, half of it drifts up, half down) or Two opposite lobes: switch on K2. Without the A term the charge that ends on U vs V is wrong, because the truncated harmonics do not reproduce the sharp "above/below the wire" step; with A (K2A) the split is restored.
4. Far from the wire. Preset Far from the wire (median height 20 µm): early signals are much smaller; the radial approximation behaves differently. Real clouds have r_eff ≈ 17.5 µm (≈ 2.5 µm above the surface) with a spread between clouds of only 0.17 µm; real K2A refuses clouds outside its radial grid (15–135 µm).
Also lost, but not shown here: the spread of birth times within one primary (one time per cloud; ≤ 0.1 % after shaping in real events) and the spread along the wire (≈ 12 µm, negligible against 1.7 mm strips).

Limits of this page

  • "Ion by ion" uses the table, interpolated linearly in angle (5° nodes) and in ln r; the real full calculation drifts every ion on its own path. In real events the table itself agrees with the full calculation to about 0.24 %.
  • Only the central anode, ions sampled from simple lobe shapes; real avalanches come from the archived Garfield++ simulation. Ion positions here are illustrative.
  • The real K2A table has 14 radii (15–135 µm), 3 anodes and 200 time nodes up to 8 µs; this page uses a coarser table built for it (10 radii, 48 times). The comparison isolates the compression, not the production table's interpolation.
  • Same provisional physics as the full calculation: Ar⁺ in pure Ar mobility (time scale provisional), electron term as a prompt step, idealised cell, B = 0. Shaping is a CR-RC³ stand-in, not the VMM3a response.
